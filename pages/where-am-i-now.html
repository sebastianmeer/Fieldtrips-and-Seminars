Where Am I Now

These learning journals helped me see where I currently stand as an IT student. I am still building my technical foundation, but I now understand that career growth in technology also depends on research curiosity, adaptability, analysis, communication, ethics, and continuous practice.

What Made the Most Impact Across Everything?

The strongest lesson is that learning must become active. I cannot stop at reading modules, attending seminars, or finishing certificates. I need to practice, build, test, revise, and explain what I learned. Python gave me a foundation for code, the Innovative Research Horizons seminar showed me how AI research uses real tools, and the professional modules gave me a clearer way to think before building.

What Does This Mean for Me as an IT Student?

Core Skills Developed

I am developing basic programming logic through Python, along with stronger skills in problem definition, critical analysis, AI awareness, data interpretation, and communication. These skills help me see software as a complete system, not just a set of code files.

How I Think and Learn Now

I now value a slower and more deliberate process. I want to understand requirements first, verify information before trusting it, and use feedback as a way to improve. I also see AI as a useful assistant that still needs human judgment.

Why Not Now?

  • Build small Python projects: I will practice consistently so the basics become natural and easier to apply.
  • Practice Jupyter Notebook: I will explore Jupyter Notebook and Anaconda so I can become more comfortable with data and AI-related workflows.
  • Create a stronger portfolio: I will document projects that show programming, data, and problem-solving skills.
  • Improve data and visualization skills: I will learn to present technical findings in a way that supports clear decisions.
  • Use AI responsibly: I will use AI for brainstorming and drafting while checking final outputs myself.
  • Learn systems connections: I will study APIs, cloud tools, databases, and cross-platform communication so I can understand how larger applications work.

I realize now that progress in IT is not measured only by what I know, but by what I can build and improve. Every seminar and course matters more when I use it in a real project. Moving forward, I will keep focusing on practical output, careful thinking, and continuous learning so my skills grow with the industry.
